## The Spec

- **Reference product:** [F2] Orisym, iOS, English — a catalogue of forest
  and sprint map symbols with tests and statistics. orient copies its shape
  (catalogue, then test) and drops what the brief drops (statistics, native
  app). It adds Italian and the control descriptions deck.
- **Also seen:** [F3] iknow-O, web, English — quizzes on control descriptions
  and map symbols by level; Quizlet community decks; club drills (Octavian
  Droobers, PurposeGames) on older ISOM editions; printable sheets
  (Maprunner). All English, several on superseded editions.
- **The differentiator** is not a feature. It is: Italian names and
  definitions taken verbatim from the official FISO / Swiss Orienteering
  translations of the current editions; control descriptions and map
  symbols in one place; nothing to install and nothing to sign into.

## The Reasoning

The survey at the gather round found plenty of drill tools and no Italian
one. That is the whole case. Orisym is the closest in shape and the brief
names it as the model, so we treat it as the reference: where the brief is
silent, "what would Orisym do" is a reasonable first answer, and "does the
brief say no to that" is the second.

We deliberately did not look for a feature to beat the others on. A family
tool with a smaller feature list than a free app is fine if it is the only
one in the right language with the right content.

### Options Considered

1. **Use Orisym and learn the English names.** Free, exists today. Rejected
   for the reason in the challenge below.
2. **Translate an open-source quiz.** None found with current editions and
   a licence that allows it; and the content is the work, not the quiz shell.
3. **Build the small Italian one.** **Chosen.**

### The Challenge

*Why not just use Orisym?* The pictograms are language-free; what a learner
needs is recognition to meaning, in the language they think in and the
language their club talks in. English adds a translation hop to every card —
for a child a real cost, for an adult a constant small one. And the reference
apps, as far as their listings show, cover map symbols; the control
descriptions deck, the one used at every start line, is the one we could
not find in Italian at all.
